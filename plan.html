2. What the data supports right now

  • Nine hotels priced for the exact preferred week. Cheapest compliant totals before tax, lowest first: HGU New York $1,091.60 (queen) · Eurostars Wall Street $1,127 · Hyatt Place Chelsea $1,183 (king) · Arlo Midtown $1,255 (king). Pestana CR7's cheapest king is $1,327 and its $26/night resort fee is already inside that number.
  • Two hotels fail the bed rule and are labelled, not hidden: The Mayfair (full/double at its cheapest tier) and Blue Angel (full bed, windowless room category).
  • Seventy-two starred Michelin entries catalogued — 5 three-star, 15 two-star, 52 one-star — each with star status stated explicitly and a link to its Guide page. Be clear about the limits: 20 of the 72 have a verified neighbourhood and 27 of the 72 have verified weekday hours — 52 rows remain ungrouped (including Daniel and Torrisi, whose Guide-blurb clues are flagged) and 43 one-star rows still need hours read off the Guide page. Rows without that data show “Not yet grouped” and a blank hours cell rather than a guess.
  • Fifteen activities with first-party source links, including four free staples (Central Park, the High Line, the Schwarzman Building, Grand Central) that materially lower the daily spend; 1 price cell and 5 hours cells remain blank + flagged.
  • One meaningful saving is quantified: New York CityPASS is $149 per adult for five attractions whose own box-office prices sum to $307.04 per the operator's own comparison table.
  • Ten selected starred restaurants now have official menu arithmetic. Each is shown as 2 × official per-person price with its menu/FAQ/PDF link and exclusions; the other 62 rows remain blank + unverified.

5. Irregularities found while checking the repo and sources

  • The Guide's own New York filter reports 50 one-star restaurants in NYC, while its 18 November 2025 announcement lists 52 one-star names (the extra two include La Bastide by Andrea Calstier in North Salem, Westchester). Flagged, not silently reconciled.
  • Michelin's two-star set: the NYC filter says 14, the announcement lists 15 names — the fifteenth is Blue Hill at Stone Barns in Tarrytown, outside the subway network.
  • Third-party menu-price aggregators contradicted each other on identical dishes for the same restaurants. They were rejected as sources; no figure on this site comes from them.
  • Several restaurant websites are JavaScript single-page apps that return no prices to a text fetch. That is why most cost cells remain blank rather than filled; the 10 rows with fetched official menus are explicitly source-linked.
  • The Met's own transit note says the M4 from Fifth Avenue to The Cloisters takes about 1 h 25 min — so The Cloisters cannot satisfy the 1-hour rule from Midtown.
